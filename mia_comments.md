Here are some thoughts that occurred to me while reading:

- DNA methylation could cause mRNA abundance differences or instead, could be the consequence of transcriptional differences (https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14176 - from the abstract: "Notably, we observed selective binding of DNMT3B to the bodies of transcribed genes, which leads to their preferential methylation.”) In this light, DNA methylation doesn’t offer a lot of insight into the upstream determinants of gene up-regulation from the hemizygous Z.
- The potential significance of the observed X-linkage of chromatin modifiers could be better motivated and supported. It is not obvious to me that Z chromatin modifiers should be Z-linked. Couldn’t the ncRNA be sufficient for recruitment/specificity? Given this, it would be helpful to see a comparison to autosomes sort of like (I think) for enrichment of the identified motifs. Related: in flies, only MOF is X-linked, the others are all autosomal, so the DCC complex in flies, at least, doesn’t offer a precedent (but I’ve been out of this area for a long time…). I really liked the analysis of sex-specific isoforms. As above, can you look at this genome-wide and ask if there is something (statistically) special about the Z-linked ones?
- You might consider asserting that the generally low expression near LINE-enriched regions doesn’t simply mean a lower dynamic range for detected dosage compensation, leading to “escape” being an artifact.
- I am unfamiliar with how CTCF modulates dosage compensation - can you motivate this analysis more for the uninitiated?
- The significance of the pioneer factor in the B compartment was not clear to me - its job is to open chromatin so why does it necessarily make sense a priori that its heterochromatin-embedded?
- The observation that LINE1s play different role in this ZW system compared to the XX system seems sort of predicted (if one were to find a trend at all) given that the latter system is designed to silence. I wonder if you might consider reframing this section so the “opposite” pattern to the Lyon’s model is not a surprise, but instead predictable given that dosage compensation in cephs is about upregulation.
- This might be too conservative, but I found the KDM3A model rather speculative without any functional data, especially given point #1 above.
